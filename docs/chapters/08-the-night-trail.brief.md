# Chapter 8 brief: The Night Trail

*v1 · 2026-10-20 · the orchestrator's brief for the text workflow (`docs/production.md`, "The plan
for chapters 6 to 10"). Every drafter, judge, critic and editor reads this. The calls below are
Claude's under decision 46, recorded in the README; Aron can reverse any of them.*

## What the chapter is

Module-1's chapter 8: **through the tunnel, or around the Barking Field (sneak, climb, or distract the
dogs), to the weasel holes by the tracks. She overhears Reynard bragging to Snip and Snatch about the
den and the pile, and finds the stash: stolen prey and a heap of tower glass. A chase, more funny than
scary, and she gets away with proof.** Tallyheart (told) or Riffle (kept) is beside her; her worry
has its brave moment; the Barking Field route finds Hail (optional). The Count is the 4s (paws), in
the morning lesson before the trail. It is her eighth day in the Clan, and the trail is that night.

This is where the case breaks open: the thieves are seen for the first time, the "scale" is shown to
be glass, and the fox is heard saying why. Nobody solves it out loud for the Clans yet: chapter 9
gathers the remedy and lays out her case file, and chapter 10 is the Gathering.

## The budget (decision 47)

- **The plain path at most about 2,000 words** (about 22 minutes) on any one reading. The two routes
  are alternatives, so a reader reads only one; keep each route's own part short (about 250 to 350
  words) and let them meet at the weasel holes.
- **One optional scene, Hail's, at most about 300 words**, on the Barking Field route only.
- About **16 typed answers**; at most **130 frames** in a reading.
- **New cast:** Snip and Snatch (the weasels), Hail. Reynard is back (drawn since chapter 4), and the
  three dogs (drawn since chapter 2). Nobody else.
- **New places: one**, the trail's end: the railway embankment by the tracks, with the weasel holes
  and the stash. The tunnel's inside, if shown, is a camera of the same new place or of the pile's
  tunnel, not another place. The Barking Field at night is the `field` set in night light.
- **No new engine features.** Choices, typed answers, Taps, skip-counts, cards and links, sticky
  found-flags, a dragonet's page, the dream question.
- **Flags:** `foundHail` (sticky, on Hail's last screen), and the route as a choice flag
  (`ch8Route: 'tunnel' | 'field'`) only if a later chapter reads it; say so in *For us*.

## The lesson kit (the Counts, Aron's first priority)

- **Warm-up:** two questions on the 6s "to wake up your whiskers", one place adapting to the 6s fact
  that was hardest for her yesterday (continuity's "The Counts" has the avoid list).
- **The picture: paws.** Four paws on every cat: two front paws, two back paws. **A four is two
  twos** ("front paws and back paws"), the same doubling idea as the 6s. The game already draws "3
  cats, 4 paws each".
- **A skip-count Tap:** cats' paws going by, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20.
- **Ten facts on the 4s, both orders mixed. She already knows most of the fours:** 4 × 1, 2 × 4,
  4 × 3, 4 × 5, 4 × 6 (the twenty-four: "your count!"), 9 × 4 and 4 × 10 came up in chapters 1 to 7.
  **The new ones are 4 × 4, 4 × 7 and 4 × 8** (in either order: five facts). At least five of the ten
  are new ones; the rest are stepping stones she knows ("Four sevens is two twos of sevens: fourteen
  and fourteen, twenty-eight"). Leave one or two facts without a right line of their own, so a quick
  answer can be noticed.
- **Story questions (two or three), after the lesson:** the morning count, **7 × 4 or 4 × 7, twenty-
  eight in seven nights** (call 2); and one on the trail if it fits without stopping the chase (a
  riddle counts). Sums go on table 4 or a table she knows.
- Help after a miss is the sand, and the fact comes back two questions later. Never a wall; no clock.
- At bedtime the eighth claw mark is on the tree.

## Steering calls (Claude's, under decision 46)

1. **The day's spine.** Morning: the seventh count. The 6s warm-up and the 4s. The day (short): the
   kept cat tells Riffle about the tunnel (ch7's promise); the told cat and Tallyheart plan. **Night:
   the trail**, by the route she chooses, to the weasel holes; Reynard's brag; the stash; the glass;
   the chase; home. Bedtime, the dream question, the hook. Keep the day short: the night is the chapter.
2. **The seventh night was robbed:** four more, from the back row again, twenty-eight in seven nights
   (4 × 7, typed after the lesson). On the told path Tallyheart heard them in the night and let them go
   on purpose ("If we pounce here, we'll never find where our prey GOES."); on the kept path the guards
   saw nothing again. Nobody is blamed.
3. **The route is her choice: "Through the tunnel" or "Around the Barking Field".** Both reach the
   weasel holes by the tracks. **The tunnel is a small cat's way:** it is just wide enough for her.
   On the told path Tallyheart is too big for it and goes round above ground, following the sounds and
   smells over their heads, and is waiting at the far end; on the kept path Riffle squeezes through
   with her (an otter pup is long and bendy). Inside, it is dark, but cats see well in the dark (true:
   say it once), roots and earth and the smell of mice, never frightening. **The Barking Field way**
   goes round the dogs' fence at night: the dogs wake (Good Boy, Drop It and a third, as README 42
   allows); a sneaking cat creeps, a climbing cat runs along the fence top, anyone else needs the dogs
   distracted, and that is where Hail is found (call 9).
4. **The worry moments.** *Small:* the tunnel is her brave moment: offered to every cat, but a small
   cat's line makes it hers ("A small cat could just squeeze in. A small cat like you."), and she goes
   in first. *Shiny:* at the stash she sees the heap of glittering glass and does not reach for it:
   she knows what it is (call 6), and that is hers. *Water* and *talk* are chapter 10's: water on the
   Moon Ferry at night (module-1's raft at night, which keeps the README rule that the open river is
   never dark), talk at the Gathering. One line each here at most.
5. **The weasel holes and the stash.** Along the railway embankment by the tracks, past the Barking
   Field, where the tiny prints were heading. **The tracks belong to no Clan** (a call: "Nobody's land.
   The trains own it."), so this is **not** past the last post, which stays the edge of fox country.
   Holes in the bank; in the biggest, the stash: prey from both Clans' piles (mice and voles: simple,
   eyes shut, no blood) and a heap of tower glass, glittering in the moonlight. The moon is eight
   evenings old, a bright half moon: the night is silver, not black.
6. **The glass.** The heap is the same smooth, clear stuff as the "scale" behind the back row. **She
   knows glass:** she lived behind a glass door all her life (her pillow-cat past: chapter 1's patio
   door). The scale was never a dragon's: it is tower glass, left by the pile. Say it plainly once
   ("It's not a scale. It's GLASS."). An eye card.
7. **Reynard's brag.** She hides (behind a sleeper, in the long grass by the bank, with her friend)
   and overhears him with the weasels: once the Clans chase the dragon away at the Gathering, the dry
   den under the Old Bridge is his, and their pile is his weasels'; the shiny glass made them all say
   DRAGON; "Nobody saw a thing." An ear card. He is a charming villain, vain and pleased with himself,
   never frightening: he never sees her, never comes near her, and leaves before the chase. Nobody
   charges him yet: that is the Gathering's.
8. **Snip and Snatch,** the weasels, named at last (decision 21's names; the README notes Snip's echo
   of a Warriors prefix). Sister and brother, skinny and quick, always squabbling over who carries
   more; funny, greedy, not cruel. They are the "two skinny shapes", the "clever little paws", the tiny
   prints. Seeing them is the first time anybody says **weasel**.
9. **Hail (optional, Barking Field route only).** In the hollow log far inside the fence (drawn there
   since chapter 2). White with grey speckles, bouncy, full of questions; the dogs think she is a
   strange dog. She leads the dogs in circles so the player can slip past. She greets her with a riddle
   (a question, of course) on a fact not used as a riddle yet (2 × 5, 6 × 5, 7 × 5, 4 × 10, 7 × 1 and
   7 × 3 are taken; a 4s fact is best), and sends Sprinkle "five". Her card is gated by `foundHail`:
   what she saw (two skinny shapes squeezing under the fence every night, toward the tracks). Her page
   goes in the book. Saying no sets nothing.
10. **The chase, funny, with proof.** The weasels spot her (a sneeze, a slip, Snatch's sharp nose) and
    give chase, all squeak and bluster. She gets away with **proof**: one shard of the stash's glass
    (an eye card can carry it), or the card for what she saw. The chase ends in a joke the kids know:
    the Barking Field dogs see the weasels off ("BYE, RUDE SQUIRRELS!"), or Hail leads them, or the
    tunnel is too tight for a weasel carrying a mouse. Nobody is hurt; nobody is caught.
11. **Home, and the kept path's Tallyheart.** *Told:* Tallyheart is beside her all night (round above
    the tunnel, or with her round the field). *Kept:* Riffle walks the trail with her, and when she
    comes home **Tallyheart is waiting** by the pile, more worried than cross, as she was under the
    bridge in chapter 2 ("I'm not angry. I was scared."; she counts her apprentice's pawsteps). She sees
    the proof and hears about the fox and the stash; **the dragonet stays the player's secret** on the
    kept path (when the kept path tells is chapter 9's). Nobody's night ends with a secret trip no
    grown-up knew about.
12. **The case, kept unsolved for the Clans.** Tallyheart (told) says it goes to the Gathering, with
    the whole story: "Every Clan should hear it." Glintstar's ruling stands until then. No new sum here
    beyond the lesson's story questions.
13. **The Case File: and her nest is full.** At most four new cards (the glass, the brag, the stash
    if separate from the glass, Hail's), each one clear thing she saw (an eye) or was told (an ear), and
    the links that make the case (the glass touches the "scale"; the brag touches the dry den and the
    fox's FLEW). Her nest holds 24 pieces of bark at most today: the spec decides how it grows before
    the build. Nothing may require `my-count` (it can be missing).
14. **The warrior thread:** walking a trail at night with her mentor (told) or her friend (kept), and
    keeping her head at the stash. One light, warm moment; no promise.
15. **The hook** for chapter 9 (*Featherlight*): Sprinkle has to fly by the full moon, and her wing is
    still hurt. Curious, never scary.
