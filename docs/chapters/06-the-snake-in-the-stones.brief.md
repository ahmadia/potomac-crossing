## What the chapter is

Module-1's chapter 6, as amended: **Coil trades a secret for being left alone: small diggers come
and go under the pile. The ReedClan patrol blames CrystalClan, then admits their own pile was
raided. Tallyheart sets guard patrols of three around the pile (the 3s). That night she dreams of a
starry cat on the Sky River: "Count what's missing, little one. Then ask who was there to see it."
Optional: following the fox trail finds Sleet.** It is her sixth day in the Clan. Chapter 7 (*The
Counts Don't Lie*) comes next: the guarded pile is robbed anyway, and the sum (24 gone against a
dragon's 60) turns the case.

## The budget (decision 47, 2026-10-18: up to 22 minutes)

- **The plain path at most about 2,000 words** (chapter 5's plain path was 1,965, about 22 minutes).
- **One optional scene, Sleet's, at most about 300 words**, with room for a full riddle.
- About **16 typed answers** on the plain path; at most **130 frames** with Sleet.
- **New cast:** Coil, the ReedClan patrol (one speaker, two who barely speak), Sleet. Nobody else.
- **New places:** at most one, and better none. Coil lives in the Old Bridge's stones (the `bridge`

- **No new engine features.** The story uses what chapters 1–5 built: choices, typed answers, Taps,

- **Flags:** `foundSleet` (sticky, on the last screen of Sleet's scene) and at most one new choice
  flag, only if a later chapter will read it.

## The lesson kit (the Counts, Aron's first priority)

The shape chapters 2–5 built, unchanged; the drafts supply the words.

- **Warm-up:** two questions on the 9s "to wake up your whiskers". One place adapts to the 9s fact
  that was hardest for her yesterday.
- **The picture:** a patrol is three cats. "How many cats in four patrols?" The game already draws
  patrols of three, walking.
- **A skip-count Tap:** patrols going by, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15.
- **Ten facts on the 3s, both orders mixed.** The idea to teach: **she already knows half the
  threes.** 3 × 1, 3 × 2, 5 × 3, 10 × 3 and 3 × 9 came up in chapters 1–5, and a fact turned
  round is the same fact (three forepaws of five claws, or five patrols of three cats: fifteen
  either way). The new ones are 3 × 3, 3 × 4, 3 × 6, 3 × 7 and 3 × 8: at least six of the ten
  facts come from these five, in either order, and the others are known ones used as stepping
  stones ("You know 3 × 5 is fifteen. One more patrol: hop three.").
  Hop by threes, like the twos.
- **Two or three story questions** that borrow from the lesson: tonight's guard patrols around the
  pile, ReedClan coming in threes. Not 7 × 2, 9 × 2 or 4 × 5 (chapters 3 and 5 both asked them).
  The dragonets' Counts so far are 2 × 5 and 6 × 5 (Sprinkle's), 7 × 5 (Squall), 4 × 10 (Puddle)
  and 7 × 1 (Wisp): Sleet's riddle must be a different fact, and a 3s fact is best.
- Help after a miss is the sand, and the fact comes back two questions later. It is never a wall.
  There is no clock. Tallyheart is warm and specific, and says why the Counts matter once.
- At bedtime the sixth claw mark is on the tree.

## Steering calls (Claude's, under decision 46)

1. **The day's spine.** The morning count (twenty gone in five nights, said; the old tom asleep on
   his pinecone). The warm-up and the 3s. Then **her first border patrol**: Tallyheart, her, and a
   third cat, because a patrol is three (Murmurchime, a warrior now, suits call 12). The ReedClan
   patrol is met on it. Coil is under the Old Bridge, on her visit to Sprinkle (with Riffle). Sleet
   is offered at the Barking Field's far corner, on the patrol or on the way to the bridge,
   whichever keeps call 9. Guards for the pile are set at sunset, then bedtime, the dream and the
   hook. The drafts may reorder the middle if the story is better for it.
2. **Coil is funny-creepy, never frightening.** He is a water snake (the real Potomac kind, not
   venomous), very polite, very slow, and a little too interested in everybody's business. He says
   hello before anyone has seen him. **Two true snake facts carry him:** a snake has no eyelids, so
   he never shuts his eyes (Patience's tip), and a snake feels footsteps through the ground. That is
   how he knows things: he felt the small diggers, every night, under the pile. He never strikes,
   squeezes or shows fangs. Riffle is jumpy and makes jokes about it; no frame has him hugging his
   tail in fright. Sprinkle thinks Coil is rude.
3. **Coil's price is being left alone.** The bridge has got noisy: pebbles, sniffling, fish, an otter
   who never stops talking. He trades the secret for quiet (no juggling under the bridge: "Otter's
   honor"). He has always known about Sprinkle and has told nobody, because nobody asked, and **he
   never trades with foxes** ("Foxes never leave anyone alone"). Say this plainly, so no child worries
   that he will give Sprinkle away. He is a red herring and innocent.
4. **Tallyheart and Coil.** Her rule ("not one paw under the Old Bridge") was made because of him. On
   the told path she has been under the bridge for three days and never met him, and he has watched
   her every time ("The ginger one. She counts her steps."). On the kept path she is not there.
5. **ReedClan.** Three cats meet her patrol at the border. "They always come in threes": Tallyheart's
   patrols of three borrow the idea. **They blame CrystalClan:** a CrystalClan apprentice was on their
   marsh yesterday, on a raft, looking at their mud, and they want to know what CrystalClan is after.
   Some of CrystalClan suspect ReedClan of the thefts in turn. **Then they admit** what their pride
   had hidden: their own pile has been raided too, night after night, and left tidy. The admission is
   the scene's turn, and it comes from the fair question her cat asks, as she did at the border in
   chapter 4. So ReedClan is not the thief: somebody is robbing both Clans. **Give no number** for
   ReedClan's losses, so nothing touches chapter 7's sum. Proud and suspicious, never cruel;
   Tallyheart keeps the Accord.
6. **The speaker is Sloshbank**, a big, stern ReedClan tom whose belly is always dripping. The other
   two are "a ReedClan she-cat" and "a young ReedClan tom". If one more must be named, use Plashwick.
   (The drafts called him Bogwade; see "Names".)
7. **The guards.** At sunset Tallyheart counts out guard patrols of three around the pile (a 3s story
   question). She is on the first watch, and nothing happens on it. Nobody guesses "under".
8. **The dream gives no card.** A dream is not evidence. The starry cat says the line, and it sends her
   toward a witness who was awake when the rain stopped. Waffles was (chapter 2), and the third witness
   to the moon is chapter 7's to bring. The starry cat is unnamed and gentle, one of "the cats who came
   before" (chapter 1); it is not a cat she knows. The usual optional dream question follows, worded
   for a night when she has already dreamed something.
9. **Sleet (optional).** The fox's trail runs along the fence to the last post at the Barking Field's
   far corner. **She never steps past the last post** into fox country, and Riffle (or, on the told
   path, Tallyheart) is beside her. On the kept path Tallyheart must not see a dragonet. Sleet is the
   eldest, **a he** (the clutch is then four sisters and three brothers): silver-white, bold and
   bossy, "spying on the enemy" from the long grass, and sure he is in charge of the whole clutch. He
   greets her with a riddle, sends Sprinkle "five", and heard Reynard talk about "the dry den"
   (Reynard's own words at the border in chapter 4). `foundSleet` is set on Sleet's last screen, and
   his page goes in her book. The fox is never seen. Saying no sets nothing.
10. **The Case File:** at most four new cards, each one clear thing she saw (an eye) or was told (an
    ear): Coil's (small diggers under the pile), ReedClan's (their pile was raided too, left tidy),
    and Sleet's (gated by `foundSleet`). At most one new link between cards. Nobody says weasel,
    tunnel, glass or what the "scale" is made of.
11. **The paths, kept light.** Read `ch3Told` where it changes who is there (Tallyheart at the bridge
    and on the fox trail), `specialty` for one line or route at most, and `worry` for one line. Her
    worry's brave moment is later (chapter 8 or 10), not here.
12. **The warrior thread:** a border patrol is a warrior's work, and she is on one. One light, warm
    moment; no promise.
13. **The hook** for chapter 7 is curious, never scary: the guards are set, and tomorrow is the
    sixth count.

## Names (checked 2026-10-18, before drafting)

Checked on both wikis through their own APIs (`warriors.fandom.com`, `wingsoffire.fandom.com`),
three ways: the exact page title, titles beginning with the name, and the exact phrase in full text.
The Warriors wiki's Names page lists prefixes and suffixes, and was read for both halves of each name.

- **Clear, whole name and both halves on both wikis:** Sloshbank, Plashwick, Wadebank, Ploppaw. Use
  only these for any new cat. **Not clear after all**, found by the drafts' names check: Bogwade
  (Wings of Fire has a page "Bog", the MudWing that Chameleon pretends to be in *Darkness of
  Dragons*), and Damplark and Boglark (Lark is a character in both series). Don't use other compounds: most marsh words are Warriors
  prefixes (Mud-, Lily-, Ripple-, Frog-, Sedge-, Moss-, Duck-), and "-whistle" is a suffix there.
  Cattail is a Wings of Fire dragon.
- **Coil:** no page on either wiki.
- **Sleet:** no page on either wiki, but the Warriors Names page lists "Sleet-" as a name prefix
  (from a post on the official *Warrior Cats* Instagram; no cat in the books has it; chapter 5's
  check found it too). This is the same kind of echo as Puddle, and it waits with Puddle's question
  in the README. The name stays.
- **Words:** "fox country", "the dry den" and "the long grass" are plain phrases on both wikis (the
  full-text hits use them as ordinary words). "Starry cat" turns up in the Warriors wiki's chapter
  summaries, describing that series' ancestors in dreams: module-1's starry cat is the same plain
  description, so keep it a description and never give the starry cats a name of their own. Their
  home is the Sky River, on screen since chapter 1; coin no word for it or for the dream.
- **Name nobody else.** A new name that is truly needed is reported in the draft's summary, and it is
  checked on both wikis before the synthesis (the workflow does this).
