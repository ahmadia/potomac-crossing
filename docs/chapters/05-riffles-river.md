- *Why the 9s are taught this way (steering 7 asked for the clearest, and the reason). Three ways were
  weighed. Hopping by nines (9, 18, 27…) is how the 2s, 5s and 10s were taught, but adding nine crosses
  a ten at every hop, so the hop itself is the hard part, and an 8-year-old ends up counting on her
  claws. The elders’ claw trick (tuck the fourth claw for 4 × 9, and read the claws on each side of it)
  gives answers without a reason, and it would make a tucked claw mean two things in one morning: “one
  less, for every cat” in the picture, and “which nine” in the trick. “One less than ten,” as ten claws
  with one tucked in, uses the two things she owns already: yesterday’s tens, which she hopped on these
  same cats, and counting back by ones, which she has done all her life. It is something she does with
  her paws before she does it with numbers (the five taps: fifty, and five claws curl in, forty-five),
  it is the picture module-1 gives the Count (a cat’s nine lives, counted on her claws: chapter 3’s
  notes proposed both forepaws with one claw tucked in, README 42 kept the nine lives, and this is the
  two as one drawing), and its two steps have two short names she can say to herself: hop, then tuck.
  The help after a miss is the same thing done slowly, so help and lesson never disagree. The digit
  pattern (“the two numbers make nine”) is kept as a check and never as the method: it arrives when two
  of her own answers are scratched in front of her (on 4 × 9’s right line or, if she missed it, on its
