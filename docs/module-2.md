## In short

- **She is a warrior now, and a warrior works.** Patrols, night watches, the Clan's frost-time store to fill and count,
  and, from her first day, **an apprentice of her own**: Chispapaw, a pillow cat from her old tower who knows every
  Count by heart in Spanish, from her little Tall One's homework, but has never caught a mouse. Tallyheart still teaches
  (decision 14), now both of them: the Counts kept fresh, and something new, take-aways.
- **The badger's track becomes a trail.** One hole a night along the river, each with strange scratches beside it, long
  ones and little pokes; the rumours grow faster than the holes (a fox, a dog, a HUNDRED claws). The scratches are the
  sand's own quick picture: **the badger counts**. Then a corner of the store falls in, and something under the Old
  Bridge starts to snore.
- **Counts and take-aways are the work.** Take-aways (never called a Count) tell her how many are missing; a Count and a
  take-away check every rumour. The store's running question, "how many more to five hundred?", is a take-away across
  two zeros: she cannot answer it until chapter 19, the chapter that teaches it.
- **Nothing is stolen this time.** The store's fallen corner rolls into a badger's bedroom, counted and uneaten; the one
  raid fails at the badger's front door. The bad guys sell badger-proof pebbles and spread rumours that can't count.
- **Collect:** seven winter sleepers the badger's digging woke, each tucked back in with a Count for a lullaby, and the
  badger page in her new book, where every rumour is crossed out by something she saw. **Earn:** a full store before
  the snow, her apprentice's first catch, a stranger's fair hearing, and a real fossil shark tooth.
- **Cheap to build:** two new sets in ten chapters (the Holt, the badger's sett); the take-aways kit (with the Hollow's
  Take-aways button) and her second book built before chapter 12; one engine feature in chapter 12 (Spanish) and one in
  chapter 14 (the Hollow's Tricky Counts), and none after.

## The secret (writer's notes, spoilers)

**What really happened.**
- **Long ago**, when the GroveClan elder was a kit and the old tom a young warrior, a young badger wintered in
  GroveClan's grove (under a fallen tree's roots, never the oak: no GroveClan cat has seen into its hollow). She ate
  every worm and never touched a mouse, and a GroveClan kit who laughed at worms taught her to scratch a count the
  GroveClan way: a square for a hundred, a line for a ten, a dot for one, the same quick picture Tallyheart draws in the
  sand. One dark night on Gathering Island she sat down in her doorway, and the old tom's patrol was in it. She said
  sorry and went home upriver at blossom-time. The Clans' scariest badger story is the old tom's telling of that night.
  The badger was Harrumph.
- **The leaf-drop storm** (module 1's storm night, the new moon) flooded her sett far upriver. The river carried her
  down on a raft of branches (Granny saw "a stump that grumbled") and left her on Gathering Island: the track in chapter
  10, old and dry at its edges because it was made that night. She dug in at the island's far end and slept ("Badgers
  sleep DEEP.").
- **The Gathering woke her** (module 1's day 14): every Clan on the river, yowling, on her island ("Too LOUD."). Half
  asleep, she heard of a pillow cat who gave a little dragon a fair hearing: "the cat who LISTENS". She set off down the
  bank to find a dry winter bed, and that cat.
- **One test hole a night**, and beside each, her supper counted the GroveClan way: GroveClan's woods (too TANGLED; one
  line, eight dots), ReedClan's bank at the wall's end (too WET; two lines, three dots), a sniff behind CrystalClan's
  new store (cats on watch; a sneeze; she went on), the Holt's bank (too SPLASHY; one line, six dots). Each hole turned
  a sleeper out into the cold.
- **The weasels, past the last post, heard the otters' gossip** and found that fear pays: badger-proof pebbles, one
  mouse each, or something shiny.
- **On the railway bank, nobody's land, Reynard met her.** Polite as ever, he told her of the best den on the river,
  under the Old Bridge: "Empty. Warm. They said you're WELCOME. No need to ask. The cats? Only minding it. For you." He
  knew CrystalClan's store was there, and that she had smelled its watch. His plan: the badger moves in, the cats panic
  and run, and five hundred pieces of prey are his, a winter without hunting. To hurry the panic he set Snip and Snatch
  shouting rumours that can't count (a HUNDRED claws; 811 mice in a night; as big as the Old Bridge). Princess Waffles
  saw the fox and "a big striped CUSHION" chatting by the trains.
- **The Barking Field** (too LOUD; two lines, one dot), then the Old Bridge: she dug in from behind, under the store's
  back wall, made her bed and slept. Her digging let the wall slump (chapter 16's crunch): thirty-seven pieces rolled
  into her room, and she scratched three lines and seven dots beside them ("Counted them. So nobody says I ATE any.").
- **Plan B (chapter 19):** with the cats afraid of the badger, the weasels are sent to empty the store before the snow,
  in through the badger's own tunnel at the back. The grey tom, too nervous about his Counts to sleep, sees them and
  yowls; from below a huge grumpy voice demands "WHO is in my HOUSE?", and the weasels run. The badger the fox sent
  becomes the store's best guard: no fox goes near a badger's den.

## For grown-ups: the real stone tooth, and a real walk

**The tooth.** Harrumph's gift is a **fossil shark tooth**: black or grey-brown, glossy, shaped like a little arrowhead.
Teeth like it wash out of the cliffs along the lower Potomac, from millions of years ago when the sea covered this land,
and module 1 already named the place: **Fossil Beach at Westmoreland State Park**, on Virginia's Northern Neck, a family
trip (check the park's current rules first: picking up what lies on the beach is the usual allowance, digging into the
cliffs is not). Purse State Park, on the Maryland side, is known for teeth too. If a trip is not possible, a small real
tooth costs a dollar or two at a museum shop or rock shop. The game's last page shows the tooth beside the mist stone,
so when you hand her a real one she'll know exactly what it is. Tallyheart's tiger's eye waits for a later module.

**A walk.** The Holt is Four Mile Run, where the stream meets the Potomac by the airport: a walk along its trail to the
river is the Holt's own place. Otters have come back to this stretch of the river; seeing one is luck, so look for
slides and tracks in the mud. Badgers don't live on the Potomac (the American badger's home is the plains and the Great
Lakes), which is why, in this world, the Clans hardly know them. Harrumph lives like Europe's badgers, who eat mostly
earthworms and dig tidy toilet pits; America's badger hunts ground squirrels.

**Spanish voices.** Chispa's lines sound best with a Spanish voice installed on the iPad (Settings, Accessibility,
