# Module one: *The Dragon Under the Bridge*

*Solo · ten chapters of about 15–20 minutes · v0.4 · 2026-10-03*
*In the game the player is "you"; these notes say "she" because Lissa is the first player.*

## Back cover

> For moons you've watched the wild cats of CrystalClan through the glass. The night you join
> them, the great leaf-drop storm hits, and something huge crashes into the river under the Old
> Bridge. By morning, prey is missing from the Clan's pile, a glittering scale lies beside it,
> and every cat in camp is whispering one word: *dragon.* Only you have seen her up close: a
> dragonet no bigger than a heron, shivering in the dark with a hurt wing and a smooth grey
> pebble in her claws. The storm scattered her six brothers and sisters all along the river. A
> smiling fox says he saw her steal the prey. At the full-moon Gathering the Clans will drive her
> out, unless a pillow cat with one moon to prove herself can find out who really did.

## Cast

- **You:** a pillow cat from the glass towers who joins CrystalClan, named by the player (these
  notes use Minnowpaw; see "Naming her cat" below). In chapter 1 she also chooses she-cat or tom,
  her fur and eyes, what she did all day as a pet (which sets what she's good at: noticing,
  sneaking, climbing, swimming, making friends) and one worry (too small, scared of deep water,
  talks too much, distracted by shiny things).
- **The Tall One:** the Tallwalker she lived with. She lets her go, and leaves the patio door open
  and a dish of water on the step.
- **Princess Waffles:** her old neighbor, a pillow cat nineteen floors up who has never touched
  grass and never stops talking. Narrates Clan life like a soap opera, calls her by her pet name,
  and turns out to be a witness ("From up here, darling, I see *everything*").
- **Tallyheart:** her mentor, a big ginger tabby she-cat with a torn ear who counts everything
  out loud: pawsteps, sparrows, stars. Her mother named her Tallykit because she kept counting her
  littermates, and she earned -heart for chasing a fox out of the nursery. Warm, funny, never
  impatient. She teaches the Counts.
- **Riffle:** an otter pup from the Holt, and her best friend. Chatters nonstop, juggles pebbles,
  knows every rumor on the river.
- **Drizzle and her clutch:** seven Mistscale dragonets scattered by the storm (below).
- **Glintstar:** CrystalClan's leader, a silver she-cat with eyes the color of the river in
  winter. Fair, careful, pushed by the other Clans.
- **Thymeleaf:** CrystalClan's medicine cat.
- **Russet:** a charming fox. The culprit.
- **Snip and Snatch:** weasel siblings who do Russet's sneaking.
- **Coil:** the water snake in the Old Bridge's stones. Trades secrets.
- **Patience:** the heron at Roaches Run. Sees everything; talks if you bring her a fish.
- **A ReedClan patrol:** suspicious neighbors.
- **The Mistscales:** the clutch's family, at the very end.

## Naming her cat

Every player's cat was born a pillow cat, so she has two names, and the player chooses both.
- **Her pet name** is what her Tall One calls her, picked from a silly list (Muffin,
  Snickerdoodle, Mittens, Biscuit, Sir Pounce-a-lot) or typed. Glintstar repeats it with
  tremendous dignity, and Princess Waffles never stops using it.
- **Her Clan name** comes when she joins ("Pillow names don't hunt"). She types the first part,
  anything she likes, and Glintstar names her Moonpaw in front of the whole Clan, in our own
  words, not the books'. If she types "Moonpaw," she stays Moonpaw, not Moonpawpaw. Suggestions
  (Minnow, Fern, Sedge, Frost, Pebble…) appear only if she asks. The Clan welcomes a new name by
  purring it.
- From then on the Clan name is everywhere: in Tallyheart's lines, over her nest, on her book's
  cover.
- When warrior names come in a later module, Glintstar will suggest endings drawn from what she
  did, and she can take one or make up her own.

## The clutch: seven dragonets

The storm scattered a clutch of seven Mistscale dragonets, all named for the weather. Drizzle
is the one everybody meets. The other six hide along the river, and she finds them by playing:
taking a path, using what she's good at, or going back to look. Each one is a single short
scene. Each greets her with a riddle that's secretly one of the Counts she already knows
(Thunder: "Five claws on each front foot. How many claws?"), and each can help with the case.

| Dragonet | Looks and manner | Hiding | Found by | Helps by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Drizzle** | mist-grey, ridged scales; gentle, shy, homesick | under the Old Bridge with a hurt wing | everyone, chapter 3 | being the heart of the story; she can't fly, which breaks Russet's lie |
| **Thunder** | slate with yellow flecks; the smallest, with the loudest voice | a glass-tower rooftop, roaring at skymonsters he thinks are giant dragons | climbing, or following the roars (ch. 4) | from up high he's watched two skinny shapes carry prey toward the tracks |
| **Puddle** | blue-grey with too-big feet; clumsy and funny | under the otters' ferry raft, the Holt's secret | noticing the raft rides low, or Riffle letting it slip (ch. 5) | saw "something with clever little paws" by the pile on the storm night |
| **Fog** | so pale she's nearly see-through; silent | standing still in the Roaches Run reeds, copying Patience | waiting with Patience long enough (ch. 5) | saw the fox drop something glittering in the reeds |
| **Sleet** | silver-white, the eldest; bold and bossy | the long grass of fox country, "spying on the enemy" | following the fox trail (ch. 6) | heard Russet talk about "the dry den" |
| **Hail** | white with grey speckles; bouncy, full of questions | a hollow log in the Barking Field, where the dogs think she's a strange dog | taking the Barking Field route (ch. 8) | leads the dogs in circles so you can slip past |
| **Dew** | pearly green, the tiniest; sweet and sleepy | an oak hollow in GroveClan's woods | gathering oak moss (ch. 9) | knows the moss that helps a dragon's wing |

Places she's been stay open between chapters, so a dragonet she missed can still be found,
right up until the full moon. All seven get home at the end either way. The ones she found say
goodbye to her by name; the others are shapes in the mist, to be met another time. If she found
all seven, the eldest Mistscale bows low to her before the mist closes: a small extra that the
story never depends on.

## The case (writer's notes, spoilers)

**What really happened.** Russet wants the dry den under the Old Bridge before frost-time,
and he would rather steal than hunt. The storm hands him a plan: a dragonet has crashed under
the bridge, so blame her. Each night Snip and Snatch come up through a tunnel under
CrystalClan's prey pile and carry off four pieces. Russet leaves a shard of tower glass by the
pile as a "dragon scale," then tells the Clan he saw the dragon fly off with the prey by
moonlight. If the Clans drive the dragon away, the den and the pile are his.

**What gives him away**
1. He says he saw the dragon **fly** off. Drizzle can't fly: her wing is hurt.
2. He says he saw it **by moonlight** on the night of the storm. There was no moon that night.
3. The "scale" is smooth tower glass. Drizzle's scales are grey and ridged.
4. The prints by the pile have five small toes, not three big claws.
5. **The Counts.** Four pieces go missing a night: twelve in three nights. Drizzle eats ten
   fish at a meal, so three nights of dragon meals would be thirty.

**Red herrings:** the ReedClan patrol (seen near the border, but their own pile was raided
too), Coil the snake (creepy, but innocent of this), and Drizzle herself.

## Paths: one culprit, different journeys

Every chapter splits and comes back together. Choices change which scenes she sees and how
she finds each clue, but every chapter ends in the same place. What varies:

- **What she's good at** opens different routes to the same clue (table below).
- **Which dragonets she finds** (the clutch, above).
- **Tell or keep the secret** (chapter 3). If she tells Tallyheart, her mentor helps hide
  Drizzle and walks the night trail with her. If she keeps it, she sneaks food to Drizzle,
  nearly gets caught, and walks the night trail with Riffle.
- **Which witness first** (chapters 5–6): the ferry otters, Patience, Coil, the ReedClan patrol.
- **Her worry becomes her brave moment.** Too small: she fits through the weasels' tunnel.
  Scared of deep water: she crosses on Riffle's raft at night. Talks too much: her big speech
  at the Gathering. Distracted by shiny things: she's the one who knows the "scale" is glass.

Her warrior name waits for a later module, and what she does this moon will be part of it.

| Clue | Ways to find it |
|---|---|
| Drizzle can't fly | Known from chapter 3. Linking it to Russet's lie: noticing (ch. 4), Riffle (ch. 7), or Tallyheart (ch. 7, if told) |
| No moon that night | Noticing; Patience; Princess Waffles ("I was up ALL night with my nerves"); the Sky River dream (ch. 6) |
| The scale is glass | Her pillow-cat past (she has lived behind window glass all her life); noticing or the shiny-things worry; Riffle; Drizzle ("that's not mine"); Fog |
| Five-toed prints, weasel fur | Noticing; climbing (from the bridge beams); Patience; the ferry otters; Puddle |
| The tunnel | The too-small worry; sneaking (she watches the pile at night); Coil's tip |
| The Counts | Always, with Tallyheart, in chapter 7 |
| The stash | Always, in chapter 8 (Thunder can point the way sooner) |

## The ten chapters

**1. Through the Glass** ([full draft](chapters/01-through-the-glass.md))
- *Story:* A pillow cat in a ground-floor home in the glass towers has watched CrystalClan
  through the patio door for moons, with Princess Waffles narrating from nineteen floors up. She
  makes her character in her reflection. The Tall One opens the door, and she steps onto grass
  for the first time. She spots the thirteenth sparrow that Tallyheart missed. In camp the Clan
  doubts a pillow cat; Tallyheart vouches for her ("Judge her by what she does, not by where she
  sleeps"); Glintstar gives her one moon, until the Gathering, to show she belongs. She chooses
  her Clan name and the Clan purrs it. Her first sight of the Sky River, her first night in the
  apprentices' den, the storm, and the crash.
- *Counts:* why the Counts matter. The 1s (tails), 8–10 answers. Her first claw mark on the
  Training Tree.

**2. After the Storm**
- *Story:* Tallyheart has her count the prey pile, stacked in pairs. Her count comes up short:
  she's the first to notice prey is missing. ("Your Counts just caught something the whole Clan
  missed.") Then her first walk around the territory: the Old Bridge, the Barking Field, and the
  Crossing, where she meets Riffle and he gives her a favorite stone. The Clan cats call the
  planes skymonsters; she knows they're airplanes, from the Tall One's glowing box, and nobody
  believes her.
- *Paths:* look under the Old Bridge first (forbidden!) or go down to the river with Riffle,
  where something on a tower roof roars back at a skymonster.
- *Counts:* the 2s (ears). Story use: counting the pile in pairs.

**3. Under the Old Bridge**
- *Story:* She finds the dragonet: hurt wing, can't fly, starving, homesick. They become
  friends. Drizzle says she usually eats ten fish at a meal, and that the storm scattered her
  six brothers and sisters along the river. Will she look for them?
- *Paths:* tell Tallyheart, or keep the secret.
- *Counts:* the 5s (claws). Drizzle counts claws with her.

**4. The Glittering Scale**
- *Story:* More prey gone, and a "scale" by the pile. Russet turns up at the border, smiling:
  he saw the dragon fly off with it by moonlight. Glintstar gives the dragon until the full moon.
  She opens her case file.
- *Paths:* if she's good at noticing, she catches "fly" right away; everyone else catches it
  later. Climbing the towers, or following the roars, finds Thunder.
- *Counts:* the 10s (both forepaws). Story use: Tallyheart's new rule stacks the pile in rows of
  ten ("then a gap shows in a heartbeat"), and she counts the rows.

**5. Riffle's River**
- *Story:* Riffle rafts her through the Roar (the courage test) to question witnesses: the
  gossiping ferry otters, and Patience, who talks only for fish.
- *Paths:* which witness first; routes to the prints and the fur. The raft rides low because
  Puddle is hiding under it, and Patience's reeds hide Fog from any cat who won't wait.
- *Counts:* review of the 1s, 2s, 5s and 10s. Story use: paying Patience ("two fish for every
  answer").

**6. The Snake in the Stones**
- *Story:* Coil trades a secret for being left alone: small diggers come and go under the pile.
  The ReedClan patrol blames CrystalClan, then admits their own pile was raided. That night she
  dreams of a starry cat on the Sky River: "Count what's missing, little one. Then ask who was
  there to see it."
- *Paths:* following the fox trail into the long grass finds Sleet.
- *Counts:* the 3s (patrols). Story use: Tallyheart sets guard patrols of three around the pile.

**7. The Counts Don't Lie**
- *Story:* Prey vanishes even from a guarded pile. She works the numbers with Tallyheart:
  four pieces a night for three nights is twelve. But Drizzle eats ten at a meal, and three
  nights of that would be thirty. And Drizzle can't fly, so Russet lied. Then she finds the tunnel.
- *Counts:* the 3s again, mixed with earlier Counts. The Counts become evidence. ("A warrior who
  knows her Counts can't be fooled.")

**8. The Night Trail**
- *Story:* Through the tunnel, or around the Barking Field (sneak, climb, or distract the
  dogs), to the weasel holes by the tracks. She overhears Russet bragging to Snip and Snatch
  about the den and the pile, and finds the stash: stolen prey and a heap of tower glass. A
  chase, more funny than scary, and she gets away with proof.
- *Paths:* Tallyheart or Riffle beside her; the worry moment. The Barking Field route finds
  Hail, who leads the dogs in circles.
- *Counts:* the 4s (paws), in the morning lesson before the trail.

**9. Featherlight**
- *Story:* Drizzle has to fly by the full moon. Thymeleaf makes a remedy for her wing, and she
  gathers one ingredient from each territory: reed root from ReedClan, oak moss from GroveClan,
  river mint from the Holt, dew from the glass towers. Then she lays out her case file for the
  Gathering.
- *Paths:* gathering the oak moss finds Dew, who knows the moss that helps a dragon's wing.
- *Counts:* the 4s again, mixed. Story use: remedy doses ("four drops a day for five days").

**10. The Gathering**
- *Story:* On the Moon Ferry upriver, Tallyheart runs the Counts with her to steady her nerves.
  On Gathering Island, Russet makes his case, and she answers each claim with a clue from her
  file (she picks which; a wrong pick gets a gentle nudge, never a fail). Snip and Snatch start
  blaming each other and confess, and the foxes are chased off the territories. Then the mist
  rolls in: the Mistscales have come for their clutch. Drizzle's wing holds, and the dragonets
  she found come to stand beside her before they fly (if that's all seven, the eldest Mistscale
  bows low to her). Drizzle presses the grey pebble into her paws, and under the full moon it
  flashes blue and green: a mist stone, the Mistscales' thanks for saving one of their own.
  Glintstar honors her in front of every Clan: her moon is over, and she belongs to CrystalClan.
  She's still Minnowpaw, and Tallyheart tells her the warrior name is coming, with this moon in it.
- *Counts:* the Moon Ferry Counts: all six, mixed, with Clanmates cheering from the raft. Help
  is always there, and it can't be failed.
- *Hook:* a badger's track in the mud upriver.

## Tallyheart and the Counts

**The goal:** the 1s, 2s, 5s and 10s, then the 3s and 4s, as solid as possible through lots
of exposure, in about 10–20% of her play time.

| Chapter | New Count | Practice and story use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1s (tails) | 1s |
| 2 | 2s (ears) | 1s, 2s; counting the pile in pairs |
| 3 | 5s (claws) | 2s, 5s |
| 4 | 10s (both forepaws) | 2s, 5s, 10s; the pile in rows of ten |
| 5 | none | 1s, 2s, 5s, 10s; paying the heron |
| 6 | 3s (patrols) | 3s and earlier; the guard patrols |
| 7 | none | 3s and earlier; the Counts as evidence |
| 8 | 4s (paws) | 4s and earlier |
| 9 | none | 4s and earlier; remedy doses |
| 10 | none | all six: the Moon Ferry Counts |

**A lesson** takes 2–3 minutes and 15–20 answers (chapter 1's, on the 1s alone, is shorter):
- a warm-up of two or three she already knows;
- the new Count, if there is one: Tallyheart pictures it (three cats: how many ears?),
  skip-counts it with her, and asks a few;
- then a mix that leans on earlier Counts, so they keep coming back.

One or two story uses per chapter, and the dragonets' riddles, add a few more. That comes to
about 150–200 answers across the module, before any extra practice.

**Rules**
- She types the answer. Remembering it does more than picking it from four.
- Both orders: 3 × 5 and 5 × 3.
- No clock on screen. Tallyheart notices speed out loud ("You didn't even need to count!").
- After a miss, Tallyheart scratches the picture in the sand (three forepaws, five claws each),
  counts it with her, and brings that one back later in the chapter.
- The Counts never block the story. A miss always leads to help, never to a wall.
- Tallyheart's praise is specific: she remembers what was hard last time.

**Tallyheart's voice**
- "Every warrior learns two things first: the hunter's crouch and her Counts. The crouch feeds
  you. The Counts feed your whole Clan."
- "Ha! You didn't even look at the sand that time."
- "Close. Let's scratch it out together: three forepaws, five claws each…"
- "Two sunrises ago, five times seven made you stop and think. Today you knew it before I
  finished asking."
- "A cat who knows her Counts can always tell when something's missing. Remember that."
- "Even Glintstar practices her Counts. Nobody is born knowing them."

**The Training Hollow (optional).** Between chapters she can go back to any lesson she's had,
with Tallyheart, as often as she likes. Practice earns nest treasures (moss, feathers, shells,
a shiny pebble) for her nest in the apprentices' den. On the Training Tree, each Count has a
claw mark that grows deeper, then glows, as she gets surer. No numbers.

## Between chapters: camp

Camp is where the pretend play lives, with no plot pushing:
- practice the hunter's crouch and the pounce;
- groom with Clanmates and hear the gossip;
- carry moss to the elders and get an old story (the Accord, the Mistscales, badgers);
- decorate her nest;
- visit her old patio: a dish of water on the warm step, and the latest from Princess Waffles;
- visit Riffle at the Crossing;
- go back to places she's been and look for dragonets she missed;
- train in the Training Hollow.

## Her book

Each chapter ends with an optional question: "What did Minnowpaw dream tonight?" Her answer
goes in her book. At the end she has a book titled with her cat's name (*Minnowpaw's First
Moon*): ten chapters, her dream lines, a page for each of the seven dragonets (a silhouette
until she finds them, then room for her own drawing), and her drawing on the cover, ready to print.

## For grown-ups: the real mist stone

Drizzle's gift is grey until the moonlight hits it, then it flashes blue and green. In real
life that's **labradorite**. A tumbled pocket stone or palm stone runs roughly $5–20 at a rock
shop, a museum store or online. If you can, choose it in person: tilt it under a single lamp,
because the flash shows only at certain angles and some stones barely have it. A small
drawstring pouch makes it something she can carry, like an otter's favorite stone.

The game's last screen shows the stone flashing in the moonlight, so when you hand her the real
one she'll know exactly what it is. Tell her to tilt it under a lamp.

Other good stones:
- **Rainbow moonstone:** a milky glow with a blue sheen, for the full moon. A good one costs a
  little more.
- **Tiger's eye:** golden bands that shift like a cat's eye, for a couple of dollars. It could be
  Tallyheart's gift in a later module.
- **A fossil shark tooth:** the Potomac's own Fossil Beach, at Westmoreland State Park, has them.
  It makes a fine dragon's tooth, and a family trip for a later module.
