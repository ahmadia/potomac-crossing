# Chapter 7 brief: The Counts Don’t Lie

*v1 · 2026-10-20 · the orchestrator's brief for the text workflow (`docs/production.md`, "The plan
for chapters 6 to 10"). Every drafter, judge, critic and editor reads this. The calls below are
Claude's under decision 46, recorded in the README; Aron can reverse any of them.*

## What the chapter is

Module-1's chapter 7: **prey vanishes even from a guarded pile. She works the numbers with
Tallyheart: four pieces a night for six nights is twenty-four. But Sprinkle eats ten at a meal, and
six nights of that would be sixty. And Sprinkle can't fly, so Reynard lied. Then she finds the
tunnel.** The Counts become evidence: "A warrior who knows her Counts can't be fooled." It is her
seventh day in the Clan. Chapter 8 (*The Night Trail*) follows the tunnel by night to the weasel
holes by the tracks: this chapter only finds its mouth.

This is the case's turning point. Chapters 1 to 6 gathered claims and clues; here the numbers and
the prints say the dragon did not do it. **The sum must be crystal clear** to an 8-year-old reading
alone: that is the chapter's first job after the lesson.

## The lesson kit (the Counts, Aron's first priority)

The shape chapters 2 to 6 built, unchanged; the drafts supply the words.

- **Warm-up:** two questions on the 3s "to wake up your whiskers". One place adapts to the 3s fact
  that was hardest for her yesterday (never one of the old pairs another day timed: continuity's
  "The Counts").
- **The picture (a call, keeping decision 42's "whiskers on one cheek"):** Tallyheart turns her
  cheek. Close up, one cheek has **six whiskers, in two rows of three**: three on top, three below.
  "Six is two threes." The game's cats are drawn with two whiskers a side, so this is a close-up,
  "up close, you can count them", and never claimed of the drawings in other panels.
- **A skip-count Tap:** cheeks going by, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30.
- **Ten facts on the 6s, both orders mixed. The idea: she already knows most of the sixes.** 6 × 1,
  2 × 6, 5 × 6, 6 × 10, 9 × 6 and 6 × 3 (the guards: "Eighteen guards!") came up in chapters 1 to 6.
  The new ones are **6 × 4, 6 × 6, 6 × 7 and 6 × 8**: at least six of the ten facts come from these
  (in either order; 6 × 6 has one), the rest are known ones used as stepping stones. The method is
  **double the 3s**, the whisker picture's own idea: 3 × 4 is twelve, so 6 × 4 is twelve and twelve,
  twenty-four. Help after a miss hops by sixes in the sand. Pick doubles an 8-year-old can do (12, 18,
  21, 24: say how to double 21 and 24, or step from a five: "five sevens and one more seven").
- **The sum, as story questions (call 3):** after the lesson, never before it. **4 × 6** (four a night,
  six nights: twenty-four) and **6 × 10** (ten fish a meal, six nights: sixty). Then the comparison,
  said plainly: twenty-four is much less than sixty. A dragon would have taken far more, and every
  night. Two or three typed story questions in all.
- Help after a miss is the sand, and the fact comes back two questions later. It is never a wall.
  There is no clock. Tallyheart is warm and specific.
- At bedtime the seventh claw mark is on the tree.

## Steering calls (Claude's, under decision 46)

1. **The day's spine.** **Dawn: Waffles** (the dream's answer, call 4). **The sixth morning count**:
   guarded all night, and four more gone anyway, twenty-four in six nights (the hole is in the middle
   of the back row again; nobody on watch saw a thing). The 3s warm-up and the 6s. **The sum** with
   Tallyheart (call 3). **The bridge**: Sprinkle's big prints beside the thief's tiny ones (call 6) and
   the fox's FLEW (call 5). **Dusk: the tunnel** (call 7). Bedtime, the dream question, the hook. The
   drafts may move the middle if the story is better for it, but Waffles comes before the sum and the
   tunnel comes last.
2. **The sixth count.** She counts the rows herself and finds the hole before anyone says it. The
   guards are puzzled and a little cross with themselves; nobody is blamed, and the old tom's
   pinecone may make one joke. "Why is it always the back?" is asked again and still not answered
   until dusk.
3. **The sum.** Tallyheart works it with her on both paths, but **only the told path's Tallyheart
   knows Sprinkle eats ten fish a meal**. On the told path the whole sum is with Tallyheart. On the
   kept path Tallyheart does the twenty-four with her ("four a night, six nights"), and the sixty
   comes at the bridge with Riffle, who has caught every one of Sprinkle's fish. On both paths
   Tallyheart says, once and warmly, **"A warrior who knows her Counts can't be fooled."** The new card
   is her own count: an eye.
4. **Waffles, the third witness to the moon.** At dawn she goes to her old step under the tallest
   tower. Princess Waffles was up ALL night with her nerves on the storm night, and there was NO moon,
   not a sliver (her own words in chapter 2). She is a pillow cat nineteen floors up who has never
   touched grass and never stops talking; she calls the player by her pet name and nothing else. Her
   card is an ear, and it answers the fox's moon: with Patience's, two witnesses against one fox. Use
   the engine's card links (`answers` if it can carry it from this chapter, a `link` if not: the spec
   decides). Waffles is funny, a little vain, and kind.
5. **FLEW, for everyone.** At the bridge, Sprinkle tries to lift her bad wing and cannot. The fox said
   the dragon FLEW out of camp. On the told path Tallyheart says it; on the kept path Riffle does; a
   noticing cat who caught it in chapter 4 says "I knew it!" Reaching that screen sets `ch4CaughtFly`
   (sticky), so her Case File draws `fox-flew` and `cant-fly` touching ("They can't both be true.").
   "So Reynard lied" may be said; nobody charges him yet: that is the Gathering's.
6. **The prints (module-1's clue 4).** Under the bridge, beside the old drag marks, Sprinkle's own
   prints have lain in the mud since the storm night, drawn and never mentioned: big, five long claws,
   as big as a heron's foot. She
   holds her paw over one, as she did over the tiny prints in the marsh. A new eye card: Sprinkle's
   prints are huge; the thief's are tiny. Nobody says weasel.
7. **The tunnel.** At dusk, with Coil's secret (small diggers under the pile) and "always the back",
   she looks behind and under the back row and finds the mouth of a small tunnel in the dug earth,
   hidden by the bramble roots: much too small for a dragon; a small cat could just squeeze in. A
   noticing cat gets there first by the dug earth; one line each for the others is plenty. **Nobody
   goes in**, and nobody says weasel or what is at the other end. Who sees it is a choice the drafts
   make, recorded in *For us*, so that chapter 8 can keep module-1's rule (told: Tallyheart walks the
   night trail with her; kept: Riffle does). A new eye card: a tunnel under the back of the pile, too
   small for a dragon.
8. **Sprinkle** hears the good news on both paths: the numbers and the prints say she didn't do it.
   She is happy and still can't fly; module-1 has her practise flying in chapter 9. A told or kept
   cat who found Sleet may pass on his "five".
9. **The Case File:** at most four new cards (the sum, Waffles, the prints, the tunnel), each one
   clear thing she saw (an eye) or was told (an ear), and the links that make the case: the moon
   answered, FLEW against can't-fly for everyone. Nobody says weasel or glass.
10. **The paths, kept light.** `ch3Told` where it changes who knows (the sum, FLEW, the tunnel);
    `specialty` for one line or route (noticing at the tunnel; climbing may see the tracks again);
    `worry` for one line (small: "a small cat could just squeeze in" is hers to notice, and her brave
    moment is chapter 8's; water, shiny, talk: one line at most). `foundSleet`, `foundSquall`,
    `foundPuddle`, `foundWisp` only where Sprinkle's tally is mentioned.
11. **Glintstar** hears the morning count and may hear the sum. She does not change her ruling: the
    dragon's case is heard at the Gathering, and no cat goes looking for it. Whether the tunnel is told
    to the Clan tonight is for the drafts, as long as chapter 8 can still follow it by night.
12. **The warrior thread:** "A warrior who knows her Counts can't be fooled." is the chapter's warm
    moment. No promise.
13. **The hook** for chapter 8 is curious, never scary: a tunnel, and somewhere it comes out.
