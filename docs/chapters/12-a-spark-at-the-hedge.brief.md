## What the chapter is

**The day after her ceremony, {name}{ending} wakes in her warrior's nest to a warrior's work: Glintstar sets the Clan
a frost-time store to fill, five hundred by the next full moon, and its count is hers. At the hedge a small pillow cat
from her old tower is staring in and counting the Clan, in Spanish, just as she once watched through the glass. She
speaks up for her, Glintstar names her Chispapaw, and gives her to {name}{ending} as her first apprentice. Tallyheart
teaches them both something new, take-aways, and at sunset {name}{ending} counts the store's first catch. Riffle
brings a curious piece of news, and at bedtime the paw thing goes into Spanish.**

It opens module 2, so it must feel like coming home and starting fresh at once: proud (she is a warrior now, and somebody
looks up to her), warm (a newcomer welcomed the way she was), and funny (Waffles, the old tom, a pillow cat who knows
every Count and has never caught a mouse). The badger is only news; nothing is missing, nothing is frightening.

## The lesson kit

**Take-aways, step 1, and the Counts kept fresh.** The lesson is the maths study's shape in the Training Hollow,
Tallyheart teaching warrior and apprentice side by side: "You know every Count a warrior needs. Now, what to DO with
them: take-aways." ("Even warriors learn new tricks."). A take-away is never called a Count: chapter 10 said "Your LAST
new one." and "Every Count a warrior needs.", and the Counts are the times tables (decision 21).
- **The warm-up:** three Counts: her two hardest over all 54 pairs (the Warrior Counts' rule, the kit's cross-table
  warm-up), then one more. The facts adapt, so the echo does too: Chispa says the first right answer in Spanish
  (`E.esFact` of it; a twin's right answer counts, so a miss still reaches one), and "My little Tall One says these
  every night! I never knew what they MEANT." follows that echo whenever it comes. (Written out in the draft as
  "¡Siete por ocho, cincuenta y seis!" for 7 × 8.)
- **Why:** Tallyheart, "A cat who knows her Counts can always tell when something's missing. Take-aways tell you HOW
  MANY."
- **The picture:** the store's prey in **squares** (a hundred: ten rows), **rows** (ten: chapter 4's rule, "then a gap
  shows in a heartbeat") and **loose** ones; "Five hundred is five squares." In the sand, the quick way to scratch a
  count: a square, a line, a dot (not credited to any Clan: where it comes from is chapter 21's).
- **The column:** the store is empty till sunset, so Tallyheart works it once on a planned store: "Say the store holds
  two hundred fifty-four, and the Clan eats one hundred thirty-one." **254 − 131 = 123**, ones first, she typing each
  digit as Tallyheart works it (one square, two rows, three loose left). Then four problems, no spread: two digits, then
  three: 58 − 23, 76 − 42, 475 − 132, 368 − 145 (589 − 267 only if play shows it is fast; checked in
  `~/potomac-crossing-work/module-2/synthesis/check.py`: no column needs a spread, every answer different). She types
  one digit a box, right to left.
- **The story's own:** at sunset, 175 caught, 42 to tonight's pile: **175 − 42 = 133** at the store; then she reads the
  store, one square, three rows, three loose.
- The words: take-aways, loose, a row, a square, ones first. **Never "borrow"**, and no spreading yet (chapter 13's).

## Steering calls (Claude's, under decision 46; call 9 settled by decision 54)

## Format

Chapter 11's story format exactly: the title "# Chapter 12: A Spark at the Hedge", an italic header with the minutes,
"How to read this draft" (only the marks this chapter uses), `##` sections, `---` between screens, **Choose:**, **Tap**,
**Counts** (a Count she types), and one new mark, **Take away** (a column take-away she types, with its problem and what

the tokens, sound effects in italics. A whole Spanish line is marked *[es]* with its English beside it in the same
screen; a Spanish word inside an English line is in «guillemets». It ends warm in her warrior's nest.

**The story only.** No notes beyond a closing block of at most 40 lines, `*For us*`, with the words and minutes, the
Spanish lines used (with their glosses), the calls made (one line of reason each), and what chapter 13 inherits in a few
lines. Flags, the Spanish feature's design, the store camera, art needs and build notes go in the spec
(`12-a-spark-at-the-hedge.spec.md`).
